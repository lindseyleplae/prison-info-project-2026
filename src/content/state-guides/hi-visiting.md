:::key-info{title="Key info"}
**There is no statewide online scheduler.** Each facility sets its own visiting days and hours and runs its own **visitation hotline**. Confirm the schedule with the specific facility before traveling.

**Every visitor must be on the inmate's approved visitor list first.** The inmate submits a proposed list, the Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation (DCR) runs a background check, and the facility approves or denies it.

**In-state video visits are available** through GTL/ViaPath tablets during assigned times, which is the practical remote option for families who cannot travel to a facility.

**About 800 sentenced men are held out of state** at the Saguaro Correctional Center in Arizona, which has a **separate** approval, visiting, and video process — see the Saguaro section below.
:::

Hawaiʻi runs a **unified system**: the Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation operates both the jails — the Community Correctional Centers, which hold mostly pretrial and short-sentence people — and the prisons that hold sentenced felons. Because it is unified, a person's status and location can change, and someone awaiting trial may be held in the same facility system as someone serving a sentence. Visiting a person in Hawaiʻi custody generally means getting on the **approved visitor list** and then **arranging a visit with the specific facility**, since each facility sets its own days, hours, and procedures.

## Getting on the approved visitor list

Before anyone can visit, they must be on the inmate's approved visitor list. The sequence is:

:::steps
1. The inmate submits a **proposed visitor list** to the facility.
2. DCR runs a **background check** on each proposed visitor.
3. The facility **approves or denies** each visitor.
:::

Once approved, an adult visitor presents a **government-issued photo ID** at the facility on the day of the visit. The same approved-list model applies at the out-of-state facility in Arizona, where the check is routed through Hawaiʻi (see the Saguaro section).

## Scheduling a visit at an in-state facility

Hawaiʻi has **no statewide online visit-booking portal**. Visiting days and hours are set **per facility**, and each facility posts its own visitation schedule and operates a **visitation hotline**. The visitation lines listed by DCR include:

- **Halawa Correctional Facility** (Aiea, Oʻahu) — visitation (808) 485-3550
- **Women's Community Correctional Center** (Kailua, Oʻahu) — visitation (808) 266-9675
- **Oʻahu Community Correctional Center** (Honolulu) — visitation (808) 832-1633
- **Maui Community Correctional Center** (Wailuku) — visitation (808) 243-5861
- **Hawaiʻi Community Correctional Center** (Hilo) — visitation (808) 933-0522
- **Kauaʻi Community Correctional Center** (Lihue) — visitation (808) 241-3050 ext. 244
- **Kulani Correctional Facility** (Hilo, Hawaiʻi Island) — visitation (808) 933-1922

Days and hours vary by facility and change. Rather than relying on a fixed schedule, families confirm the current visiting days, hours, and any required appointment by calling the facility's visitation line.

:::callout{variant="tip"}
Confirm which facility the person is currently held at before arranging a visit. Hawaiʻi has no public DCR web locator; families use **VINELink** (the VINE/SAVIN system), searching by name or ID. See [Intake, classification, and transfers in Hawaiʻi](/states/hawaii/guides/transfers/).
:::

## In-state video visits

Hawaiʻi's communications vendor is **Global Tel Link (GTL) / ViaPath**, billed through **ConnectNetwork**. GTL **tablets** were rolled out across the in-state facilities and support **video visitation** during assigned times, alongside messaging and media. Tablets are provided to inmates at no charge; premium features such as video visits cost money.

For families who cannot travel to a facility, video visits by tablet are the practical remote option. Booking and the assigned video-visit times are handled **per facility**, so the facility's visitation line is the place to confirm how its video visits are scheduled.

## Inter-island travel

Three of the four jails and one of the four prisons are on **neighbor islands** rather than Oʻahu:

- **Maui Community Correctional Center** — Wailuku, Maui
- **Hawaiʻi Community Correctional Center** — Hilo, Hawaiʻi Island
- **Kauaʻi Community Correctional Center** — Lihue, Kauaʻi
- **Kulani Correctional Facility** — on the slope of Mauna Loa, about 20 miles southeast of Hilo, Hawaiʻi Island

These are reachable from Oʻahu mainly by **air**. For families who cannot travel between islands, the **in-state video visit by tablet** is the remote alternative.

## Where people are held by sex

**Women** in Hawaiʻi custody — at maximum, medium, and minimum custody, both sentenced and pretrial — are held at the **Women's Community Correctional Center** in Kailua, Oʻahu, the only women's prison in the state. Despite "Community Correctional Center" in its name, it is classified as a prison rather than a jail.

**Sentenced men** are classified through the island jails and **Halawa Correctional Facility**, Hawaiʻi's largest male prison, and may then be placed at the minimum-security **Waiawa Correctional Facility** (Waipahu, Oʻahu), the minimum-security **Kulani Correctional Facility** (Hawaiʻi Island), or the out-of-state **Saguaro Correctional Center** in Arizona. Visit type and scheduling at each facility follow that facility's rules.

## Saguaro Correctional Center (Arizona)

The single most distinctive feature of the Hawaiʻi system is that roughly **800 sentenced Hawaiʻi men** (down from about 1,700 in the 2010s) are held in the continental U.S. at the **Saguaro Correctional Center in Eloy, Arizona**, a private prison operated by CoreCivic under contract with Hawaiʻi. Visiting, video, and approval for Saguaro follow a **separate process** from the in-state facilities.

**In-person visiting requires travel to Arizona.** For general-population inmates, visiting days are **Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and Monday**, from **8:00 a.m. to noon and 1:00 to 4:00 p.m.** (Tuesday is reserved for restrictive-housing inmates.)

**Approval is routed through Hawaiʻi.** The inmate submits the proposed visitor list, it is sent to Hawaiʻi for an **NCIC background check**, and the facility's chief of security approves or denies it. The process takes about **2 to 4 weeks**.

:::callout{variant="info"}
Because every visitor traveling from Hawaiʻi is well beyond the facility's **300-mile** threshold, families may request **extended all-day visits** (8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.) rather than a single morning or afternoon block. Extended and special visits are requested in advance and are subject to the inmate's disciplinary status.
:::

**Video visits** for the Hawaiʻi population at Saguaro are on **Saturdays** and are booked through the **State of Hawaiʻi mainland branch at (808) 837-8022** — not through ICSolutions, which is the path used by the Idaho and Montana populations also held at Saguaro.

Because approval takes about 2 to 4 weeks, travel arrangements depend on the visitor first being approved through the Hawaiʻi NCIC process. Confirm approval and the current schedule with the facility and the mainland branch before making any commitments.

## Verify Before Acting

:::callout{variant="warning" title="Verify Before Acting"}
Confirm the plan with the specific facility before traveling. Every visitor must first be on the inmate's **approved visitor list**, and Hawaiʻi has **no statewide scheduler** — each facility sets its own days and hours and runs its own **visitation hotline**, so confirm the current schedule by calling the facility directly. Bring a **government-issued photo ID**. For the roughly 800 men held at **Saguaro in Arizona**, visiting requires travel to Arizona, approval is routed through Hawaiʻi via an **NCIC check that takes about 2 to 4 weeks**, and video visits for the Hawaiʻi population are on Saturdays through the mainland branch at **(808) 837-8022**. Confirm the person's **current facility** first, since neighbor-island and out-of-state facilities are reachable mainly by air. Visiting rules and schedules change; the facility and DCR are the authoritative sources.
:::
