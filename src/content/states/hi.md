Hawaiʻi's prisons and jails are run by the **Hawaiʻi Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation (HI DCR)**, the agency created on January 1, 2024 when the former Department of Public Safety was reorganized and its law-enforcement functions were split off. Hawaiʻi is one of a handful of states with a **unified corrections system**: because Hawaiʻi has no county jails, the state runs both the pretrial jails and the prisons for sentenced people. The agency refers to a person in its custody as an **inmate**; there is no public DCR web locator, so people are found through **VINELink** (the national VINE service; select Hawaiʻi), or by calling 1-800-247-9763.

This section details **nine facilities** — four prisons, four jails, and one out-of-state prison. The four **prisons**, which hold sentenced people, are **Halawa Correctional Facility** (Aiea, Oʻahu), the largest and main facility for men; **Waiawa Correctional Facility** (Oʻahu), a minimum-security prison for men nearing release; **Kulani Correctional Facility**, a minimum-security men's prison on Hawaiʻi Island; and the **Women's Community Correctional Center** (Kailua, Oʻahu), the only women's prison in the state. The four **jails**, officially the **Community Correctional Centers**, hold mostly pretrial and short-sentence people and also some sentenced people: **Oʻahu Community Correctional Center** (OCCC), the largest, which has held **men only** since DCR relocated all of its women to the Women's Community Correctional Center in late 2024; **Maui Community Correctional Center** (Wailuku); **Hawaiʻi Community Correctional Center** (Hilo); and **Kauaʻi Community Correctional Center** (Lihue).

**A defining feature of Hawaiʻi is that many of its sentenced men are held thousands of miles away.** About **800 Hawaiʻi men** are housed in the continental U.S. at the **Saguaro Correctional Center** in Eloy, Arizona, a private prison operated by **CoreCivic** under a contract Hawaiʻi has used since the 1990s to relieve in-state crowding. Visiting someone at Saguaro means traveling to Arizona or connecting by video and phone on a schedule that facility sets — its rules, money process, and visiting arrangements differ from the in-state facilities and are covered on its own page. Lawmakers have weighed proposals to begin returning people to Hawaiʻi, but as of mid-2026 the out-of-state contract continues.

**Geography shapes visiting** even within Hawaiʻi. The neighbor-island facilities — on Maui, Kauaʻi, and Hawaiʻi Island — are reachable from Oʻahu mainly by air, so an in-person visit can require a flight. Video visits are available through tablets at the in-state facilities, which is the practical remote option for families on another island.

Hawaiʻi **has no death penalty** — capital punishment was abolished in 1957, before statehood. No Hawaiʻi facility holds a death row.

A few features shape how families stay in touch. **Incoming personal mail goes directly to the facility** at the in-state prisons and jails — Hawaiʻi does not use an off-site mail-scanning vendor — with books and magazines accepted only directly from a publisher or approved vendor; mail to Saguaro follows that prison's own rules. Phones, tablets, and video visits run through **GTL / ViaPath**, billed through **ConnectNetwork**, which is also how money is deposited to an in-state account (a separate process applies at Saguaro). Health care is provided **in-house** by DCR's Health Care Services Division. Independent oversight runs through the **Hawaiʻi Correctional System Oversight Commission (HCSOC)**. A new Oʻahu jail to replace the aging OCCC is in the planning stage; it is noted on the OCCC page but is not yet built.
