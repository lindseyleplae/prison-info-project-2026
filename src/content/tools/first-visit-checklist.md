Every facility sets its own rules, and they change. This checklist covers what applies almost everywhere; the facility's page on this site lists its phone number, schedule, and specifics. The [First Visit guide](/guides/first-visit/) walks through the full day in detail.

## One to two weeks ahead

:::steps
1. **Confirm you are an approved visitor.** Most states require approval *before* the first visit, and processing can take weeks. The state's visiting guide on this site explains the application process.
2. **Confirm where the person is held.** People transfer, sometimes without notice. Check the state's inmate locator, linked from the state's pages here.
3. **Check how visits are scheduled.** Some facilities take walk-ins; many require an appointment booked days in advance through a scheduling system.
4. **If bringing children, check the rules for minors.** A birth certificate is commonly required, and a child visiting without their legal guardian often needs notarized consent. Requirements vary by state.
:::

## The day before

:::steps
1. **Call the facility to confirm visiting is happening.** Lockdowns and schedule changes cancel visits without notice, after families have already traveled.
2. **Lay out clothing against the dress code.** Common restrictions: no clothing resembling what incarcerated people or staff wear, no revealing or sleeveless items, no hoods, minimal metal. The state's visiting guide lists specifics.
3. **Gather identification.** A current government-issued photo ID is required nearly everywhere. Bring any documents required for children.
4. **Plan to arrive 30–60 minutes early.** Processing takes time, and most facilities stop admitting visitors at a cutoff well before visiting ends.
:::

## What to bring

- Current government-issued photo ID
- A single car key (or leave keys in a locker where provided)
- Children's required documents, if children are visiting
- A small amount of cash or coins **only if the facility allows it** — some permit a clear bag with money for vending machines, others allow nothing in; machines may also be empty or out of service, so plan to manage without

## What to leave behind

- Phones, smartwatches, and all electronics — banned in nearly every visiting room
- Purses, bags, and wallets beyond the ID itself
- Tobacco, vaping devices, and lighters
- Medication, unless declared to staff at the entrance — ask the facility ahead about anything medically necessary
- Weapons of any kind: many states prohibit them anywhere on facility grounds, including inside a locked car

## At the facility

- Arrive before the processing cutoff; late arrivals can be turned away
- Expect a metal detector and possibly a pat-down; declining screening means no visit
- Follow posted rules on physical contact — where contact is allowed, it is typically limited to a brief greeting
- Staff direction is final; a visit can be shortened or ended at the facility's discretion

## Common reasons a first visit fails

- The visitor is not yet on the approved list
- A dress-code violation at the door — a plain change of clothes left in the car covers this
- Expired or missing ID
- Arriving after the processing cutoff
- The person was transferred, or the facility went on lockdown — the day-before call catches both
