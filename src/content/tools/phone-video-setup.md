Three things about prison phones that surprise most families: calls only go **one direction** (the incarcerated person calls out; you cannot call in, and a missed call cannot be returned); every state contracts a **private company** to run the system, and you deal with that company, not the prison; and the company differs by state. The state's Phone & Video guide on this site names the provider and its rates.

## Setting up phone calls

:::steps
1. **Find the provider for the person's facility.** The state's Phone & Video guide and each facility page on this site name it — common ones are Securus, ViaPath (GTL), and ICSolutions.
2. **Create an account with that provider** using the phone number the person will call. Using your legal name, matching your visitor paperwork, avoids mismatch problems later.
3. **Fund the account.** A small first deposit is enough to test the connection. Deposit fees vary; the provider's published fee schedule is the authoritative price list.
4. **Tell the person the number.** At many facilities they must add it to an approved call list on their end before the system will connect.
5. **Answer the first call.** It arrives from an unfamiliar, often out-of-state number, and an automated message announces the call and asks you to accept — stay on the line through the prompt.
:::

## Setting up video visits

- Video visiting usually runs on a **separate system**, sometimes with its own account, even when the same company provides it. The state guide names the video provider.
- Sessions are typically **scheduled in advance** — commonly one to three days ahead — and time slots can fill quickly.
- Dress code applies on camera, sessions are monitored and may be recorded, and recording on your end is prohibited.
- Complete the provider's device and connection check **before** the first scheduled session, not during it.

## Messaging and tablets

Many states also offer paid electronic messages and photo sharing through a tablet provider. The state's Mail & Packages or Phone & Video guide covers what is available and who runs it.

## If calls stop coming

- **Check the account balance first** — an empty account is the most common cause.
- **Check whether the number got flagged as spam.** Phone carriers sometimes silently block the system's numbers; saving the number as a contact prevents this.
- **Check the locator.** A transfer can change the facility, the provider, and the account setup all at once.
- **A facility lockdown suspends calls** — this resolves on the facility's timeline, not the provider's.
